Chapter 4 covers Project Appraisal — how to evaluate whether a project is worth pursuing. It has two parts: Technical Analysis (market, technical, financial feasibility) and Financial Evaluation (NPV, BCR, IRR, Payback Period, ARR). Financial evaluation is heavily tested with numericals.

Exam focus: NPV, BCR, and IRR are the highest-value topics. Know formulas, solve numericals, and understand decision rules (NPV>0 → accept; BCR>1 → accept; IRR>cost of capital → accept). ARR is also asked.
🔧 Technical Analysis ★★☆☆

Before financial evaluation, a project must pass a technical analysis examining feasibility from multiple angles.

Analysis TypeKey Questions
Market AnalysisIs there demand for the product/service? What is the market size? Who are competitors?
Technical AnalysisIs the technology available and proven? Can we build/operate it? Do we have the expertise?
Financial AnalysisWill revenues exceed costs? What is the ROI? Can we fund it?
Economic AnalysisWhat is the broader impact on the economy, employment, GDP?
Environmental AnalysisWhat are the environmental impacts? Regulatory compliance?
Social/Political AnalysisCommunity impact, political risk, government support
💰 Net Present Value (NPV) ★★★★
NPV converts all future cash flows to their present value using a discount rate, then subtracts the initial investment. It represents the wealth created (or destroyed) by the project in today's money.
NPV Formula
NPV = Σ [ Ct / (1+r)t ] − I0
Where: Ct = cash inflow at time t  |  r = discount rate (cost of capital)  |  t = time period  |  I0 = initial investment (at t=0)

Decision Rule

NPV ValueDecisionMeaning
NPV > 0✅ AcceptProject creates value; returns exceed cost of capital
NPV = 0🤔 IndifferentProject just covers cost of capital — break even on value
NPV < 0❌ RejectProject destroys value; better to invest elsewhere

NPV Profile

Remember: NPV uses all cash flows over the project life. It is the most preferred method because it directly measures value created. Its weakness is sensitivity to the discount rate assumption.
📊 Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR) ★★★★
BCR (also called Profitability Index) is the ratio of the present value of all benefits to the initial investment. It tells you how much value you get per unit of investment.
BCR Formula
BCR = Σ [ Ct / (1+r)t ] ÷ I0
BCR = PV of Benefits / Initial Investment  |  Note: BCR = 1 + NPV/I0

Decision Rule

BCR ValueDecisionMeaning
BCR > 1✅ AcceptBenefits exceed cost — project creates value
BCR = 1🤔 IndifferentExactly break even on investment
BCR < 1❌ RejectBenefits don't cover investment
BCR vs NPV: BCR is useful when comparing projects of different sizes (e.g., BCR=1.5 vs BCR=1.2 tells you which is more efficient per dollar invested). NPV tells you absolute value; BCR tells you relative efficiency.
📈 Internal Rate of Return (IRR) ★★★★
IRR is the discount rate at which NPV = 0. It is the project's own internal rate of return — the return generated by the project's cash flows alone.
IRR Definition
Find r such that: Σ [ Ct / (1+IRR)t ] = I0
i.e., NPV = 0 when r = IRR  |  IRR has no closed-form solution — must be found by trial & error or interpolation

Decision Rule

ComparisonDecisionMeaning
IRR > Cost of Capital (r*)✅ AcceptProject earns more than it costs to finance
IRR = Cost of Capital🤔 IndifferentExactly covers financing cost
IRR < Cost of Capital❌ RejectCheaper to invest elsewhere

Interpolation Method to Find IRR

Linear Interpolation Formula
IRR = r₁ + [ NPV₁ / (NPV₁ − NPV₂) ] × (r₂ − r₁)
Where r₁ gives positive NPV₁ and r₂ gives negative NPV₂  |  Choose r₁ and r₂ close to each other
Weakness of IRR: Multiple IRRs can exist if cash flows change sign more than once. For mutually exclusive projects, NPV is preferred over IRR.
⏱️ Payback Period (PBP) ★★★☆
Payback Period is the length of time required to recover the initial investment from the project's cash inflows. Simple (undiscounted) PBP ignores time value; Discounted PBP accounts for it.
Simple Payback Period
PBP = I0 / Annual Cash Inflow (if uniform)
For non-uniform flows: cumulate inflows year by year until they equal I₀
Discounted Payback Period
Cumulate [ Ct / (1+r)t ] until sum = I0
Same idea but using present values of each year's cash flow

🏦 Average Rate of Return (ARR) ★★★☆
ARR measures the average annual profit as a percentage of the investment. There are 7 different measures depending on what "investment" and "profit" you use — Azeem sir lists all 7.
General ARR Formula
ARR = (Average Annual Profit / Investment) × 100%

7 Measures of ARR

#MeasureNumeratorDenominator
1ARR on Initial InvestmentAverage annual profit after taxInitial investment
2ARR on Average InvestmentAverage annual profit after taxAverage investment = (Initial + Salvage)/2
3ARR (before tax, initial)Average annual profit before taxInitial investment
4ARR (before tax, average)Average annual profit before taxAverage investment
5ARR on Total CapitalAverage annual profitTotal capital employed
6ARR on Net WorthAverage annual profitNet worth (equity)
7ARR (cash flow basis)Average annual cash flowInitial investment
Key weakness of ARR: Does NOT account for the time value of money. Cash flows received later are treated the same as those received earlier.
Decision rule: Accept if ARR > a predetermined hurdle rate (minimum acceptable return set by management).
🔢 Worked Math Problems ★★★★

Problem 1: NPV Calculation

A project requires an initial investment of $50,000. It generates cash inflows of $15,000 per year for 5 years. The discount rate is 12%. Should the project be accepted?
Solution
NPV = Σ [15,000/(1.12)t] − 50,000 for t=1 to 5
YearCash FlowDiscount Factor (12%)PV
1$15,0001/1.12 = 0.893$13,395
2$15,0001/1.254 = 0.797$11,955
3$15,0001/1.405 = 0.712$10,680
4$15,0001/1.574 = 0.636$9,540
5$15,0001/1.762 = 0.567$8,505
Sum of PVs$54,075
NPV = $54,075 − $50,000 = +$4,075
NPV > 0 → ✅ Accept the project

Problem 2: BCR Calculation (same project)

BCR = $54,075 / $50,000 = 1.08
BCR > 1 → ✅ Accept — for every $1 invested, we get $1.08 in PV returns

Problem 3: Payback Period (non-uniform)

Investment = $20,000. Cash flows: Year 1 = $5,000, Year 2 = $7,000, Year 3 = $8,000, Year 4 = $6,000. Find PBP.

PBP = 3 years exactly (cumulative reaches $20,000 at end of Year 3)
❓ Past Year Questions & Full Answers — AA Ch 4
NPV, BCR, and IRR numericals carry 10–15 marks and appear in almost every exam. Practice the full worked-example format.

Q1. Worked Example — Calculate NPV and BCR; decide accept/reject. [10–15 marks, ★★★★]

Problem: A project costs Tk. 1,00,000 today. Cash inflows: Yr 1 = 30,000; Yr 2 = 40,000; Yr 3 = 50,000; Yr 4 = 20,000. Discount rate = 10%. Find NPV and BCR.

Step 1 — Discount each cash flow:
PV(Yr 1) = 30,000 / (1.10)¹ = 30,000 / 1.100 = 27,273
PV(Yr 2) = 40,000 / (1.10)² = 40,000 / 1.210 = 33,058
PV(Yr 3) = 50,000 / (1.10)³ = 50,000 / 1.331 = 37,566
PV(Yr 4) = 20,000 / (1.10)⁴ = 20,000 / 1.464 = 13,660

Step 2 — Sum of PVs: 27,273 + 33,058 + 37,566 + 13,660 = 1,11,557

Step 3 — NPV: NPV = Σ PV − I₀ = 1,11,557 − 1,00,000 = Tk. 11,557
Since NPV > 0 → Accept the project

Step 4 — BCR: BCR = Σ PV / I₀ = 1,11,557 / 1,00,000 = 1.116
Since BCR > 1 → Accept the project

Q2. Worked Example — Find IRR by interpolation. [10–12 marks, ★★★★]

Method: IRR is found by trying two discount rates — one that gives +ve NPV (r₁) and one that gives −ve NPV (r₂) — then interpolating.

Formula:
IRR = r₁ + [NPV₁ / (NPV₁ − NPV₂)] × (r₂ − r₁)

Using the same project above:
At r = 10%: NPV = +11,557 (computed above)
Try r = 20%:
PV(Yr 1) = 30,000/1.20 = 25,000
PV(Yr 2) = 40,000/1.44 = 27,778
PV(Yr 3) = 50,000/1.728 = 28,935
PV(Yr 4) = 20,000/2.074 = 9,642
Σ PV = 91,355 → NPV = 91,355 − 1,00,000 = −8,645

IRR = 10 + [11,557 / (11,557 + 8,645)] × (20 − 10)
IRR = 10 + [11,557 / 20,202] × 10 = 10 + 5.72 = ≈ 15.72%

If IRR > cost of capital (say 10%) → Accept. IRR = 15.72% > 10% → Accept.

Q3. Calculate Simple and Discounted Payback Period. [5–10 marks, ★★★]

Simple Payback Period (undiscounted):
Cumulate inflows until they equal initial investment (Tk. 1,00,000):
After Yr 1: 30,000 | After Yr 2: 70,000 | After Yr 3: 1,20,000 → payback occurs in Year 3
Fraction = (1,00,000 − 70,000) / 50,000 = 30,000/50,000 = 0.6
Simple PBP = 2 + 0.6 = 2.6 years

Discounted Payback Period (using PV values from Q1):
After Yr 1: 27,273 | After Yr 2: 60,331 | After Yr 3: 97,897 | After Yr 4: 1,11,557
Payback between Yr 3 and Yr 4.
Fraction = (1,00,000 − 97,897) / 13,660 = 2,103 / 13,660 = 0.154
Discounted PBP = 3 + 0.154 ≈ 3.15 years

Q4. List and explain the 7 measures of Accounting Rate of Return (ARR). [5–8 marks, ★★★]

Answer:
ARR (also called Accounting Rate of Return) can be computed in several ways depending on what "investment" and "profit" mean:

1. Net Profit (after tax) / Original Investment × 100
2. Net Profit (after tax) / Average Investment × 100
3. Net Profit (before tax) / Original Investment × 100
4. Net Profit (before tax) / Average Investment × 100
5. Net Cash Inflow / Original Investment × 100
6. Net Cash Inflow / Average Investment × 100
7. Incremental Profit / Incremental Investment × 100 (used for comparing alternatives)

Average Investment = (Original Investment + Salvage Value) / 2
Net Profit = Net Cash Inflow − Depreciation

Limitation: ARR ignores the time value of money. It is easy to calculate but not as theoretically sound as NPV or IRR.
