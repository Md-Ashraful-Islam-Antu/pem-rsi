Chapter 3 focuses on the unique role of the Project Manager (PM) — how it differs from a traditional Functional Manager (FM), and the six special demands placed on a PM that make project management uniquely challenging.

Exam focus: The 6 special demands on a PM (from Meredith & Mantel) are asked very frequently. The comparison between PM and FM is another common question.
🆚 Project Manager vs Functional Manager ★★★★
A Project Manager manages a temporary, unique undertaking with cross-functional authority. A Functional Manager manages a permanent department with deep technical specialization.
DimensionProject ManagerFunctional Manager
Scope of workEntire project (all functions)One department/function
Authority sourceDelegated from top (often weak)Line authority (formal, strong)
Time horizonShort-term (project duration)Long-term (ongoing)
Primary concernClient satisfaction; delivery on time/costTechnical excellence; efficiency
UncertaintyVery highModerate
Success measureProject goals metDepartmental KPIs
⚡ Six Special Demands on a Project Manager ★★★★ — Very Frequent
Most important section in this chapter. Meredith & Mantel identify these 6 demands. Know all 6 with brief explanations — common 8–10 mark question.
#Special DemandWhy It's Challenging
1 Acquiring Resources PM must acquire people, funds, and equipment from functional departments — but has no direct authority over them. Must negotiate, persuade, or use influence.
2 Motivating Personnel Without Authority Project team members still report to their functional managers for performance reviews and raises. PM must motivate without the typical carrots (pay, promotion) a line manager has.
3 Dealing with Obstacles Unexpected technical problems, resource shortfalls, stakeholder conflicts, and scope changes constantly arise. PM must solve them quickly without derailing the project.
4 Making Trade-offs Between Time, Cost & Performance Rarely can all three be simultaneously optimized. PM must decide: do we sacrifice some performance to meet the deadline? Spend more to maintain quality? These are difficult judgment calls.
5 Tolerance for Uncertainty Projects are unique; outcomes are never guaranteed. PM must function effectively even when outcomes, technologies, or scope are unclear — cannot wait for perfect information.
6 Working with Conflict Conflict arises over resources, priorities, technical approaches, and schedules. PM must manage conflict constructively rather than avoiding it, since unresolved conflict destroys project performance.
🎯 Skills of an Effective Project Manager ★★☆☆
Skill CategoryExamples
TechnicalUnderstanding of the project's technical domain, using PM tools (CPM, WBS, earned value)
AdministrativePlanning, organizing, budgeting, controlling, reporting
InterpersonalCommunication, negotiation, conflict resolution, motivation, leadership
PoliticalNavigating organizational power, building alliances, managing upward
The most effective PMs are generalists — broad enough to coordinate all functions — not deep specialists. They need to know enough technical to be credible, but their primary value is coordination and leadership.

PM as an Integrator

The PM integrates all functions and stakeholders towards the single project goal.
