Chapter 2 covers how organisations are structured for project work. It explains the Line, Line & Staff, Functional, Matrix (Weak/Balanced/Heavy) and Dedicated structures, the authority spectrum between PM and Functional Manager (FM), how to choose the right structure, and the Project Team Formation (Technical + Logistics roles).

Exam focus: PM vs FM authority for each structure — with Bangladesh examples. Asked TF 23-24, 21-22, 20-21, 18-19, 17-18, 16-17, 14-15, 11-12, 10-11. Know the 4 management functions (P,O,L,C) and who does what in each structure.

Chapter Roadmap

🏢 Organisation Basics ★★☆☆

Definition of an Organisation

An organisation is an institution with three key elements:
1. People — it must have people to function
2. Specific Job Responsibilities — each person has defined roles/duties
3. Chain of Command/Supervision — there is a hierarchy of authority

Chain of Command vs Span of Control

ConceptDirectionMeaningBest Example
Chain of Command Vertical ↕ Hierarchy of authority — who reports to whom from top to bottom Army — best example of chain of command; strict hierarchy, clear reporting
Span of Control Lateral ↔ How many subordinates one manager can effectively supervise at the same level Varies by industry; skill-based
Wider Span of Control = fewer levels of hierarchy = less cost, but can damage work quality.
An officer's capability determines how many people they can effectively supervise. A highly skilled manager can handle a wider span.

Basic Organisation Structures: Line vs Line & Staff

StructureDefinitionBangladesh Example
Line Structure Normal basic chain of command. CEO → Managers → Frontline officers → Operators. All positions are within the main chain of command. Simple and hierarchical. Gazipur RMG (Ready Made Garments) factory — straightforward production hierarchy: CEO → Production Manager → Supervisor → Workers
Line & Staff Structure Special "staff" positions added OUTSIDE the main chain of command. These positions report directly to the CEO/top management but are not in the main line of authority. They advise, audit, or support. Square Pharmaceuticals: Company Secretary, R&D Manager, QA Officers, Audit Officers, Training Institute Director — all report directly to CEO outside the main production hierarchy

Project Organisation Structures — Classification

Within a company, projects can be organised in three main ways. These build on Line (→ Functional) and Line & Staff (→ Dedicated) structures:

4 Management Functions

FunctionAbbreviationWhat it means
PlanningPSetting goals, defining strategy, creating schedule and budget
OrganizingOAssigning tasks, allocating resources, structuring teams
LeadingLMotivating people, directing teams, day-to-day management
ControllingCMonitoring progress, correcting deviations from plan
These 4 functions (P, O, L, C) are shared between PM and FM depending on the structure. Know exactly who does what for each structure — this is the most common exam question.
⚙️ Functional Structure ★★★☆
Exam TF 23-24, 18-19, 17-18, 14-15, 11-12, 10-11 — "Describe Functional structure with PM/FM authority" or "Distinguish Functional from Dedicated."

What is Functional Structure?

In Functional Structure, NO separate PM is hired. The project is managed from within an existing department. The department's Functional Manager (FM) assigns extra responsibilities to existing staff to run the project alongside routine work. It is 80–90% department-oriented.

PM vs FM Authority in Functional Structure

RolePOLCNotes
FM (Functional Manager) ✓ ✓ ✓ ✓ FM is ALL IN ALL. Does all 4 management functions
PM (Project Manager) ✗ ✗ ✗ ✗ NO PM hired. Project is 80–90% department-oriented

Advantages & Disadvantages

AdvantagesDisadvantages
No extra investment — existing staff and FM handle the project Extra burden on FM and department staff (they still have routine work)
Team has existing expertise in the field (they know the department's work) Officers prioritize their routine departmental work first; project gets second priority
No new hiring needed — cost-effective Project often misses time and budget targets due to competing priorities

Bangladesh Example

BRAC Bank Payroll System upgrade: The existing IT department (FM = IT Manager) assigns some programmers to build an upgraded payroll system while they continue their regular IT duties. No separate PM is hired — the IT FM manages everything. Project is 80–90% IT department-oriented.
🏗️ Dedicated Structure ★★★☆
Exam TF 23-24, 18-19, 17-18, 14-15, 11-12, 10-11 — the other end of the authority spectrum. PM is "all in all" here.

What is Dedicated Structure?

In Dedicated Structure, a separate project department is created. A PM is hired specifically for the project, plus all team members are separately hired. High investment required. The team has full focus on completing the project within time and budget — no competing priorities. Used for long-term projects OR companies that run frequent short projects.

PM vs FM Authority in Dedicated Structure

RolePOLCNotes
PM (Project Manager) ✓ ✓ ✓ ✓ PM is ALL IN ALL. Does all 4 management functions for the project
FM (Functional Manager) advisory ✗ ✗ ✗ FM helps with technical expertise only when asked by PM

Advantages & Disadvantages

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Full focus on project — team members have no competing priorities High investment — hiring PM + all team members separately costs significantly more
Project is very likely to complete within time & budget (sole focus) When project ends, team disbands — no job security
Clear authority — PM has full control Duplication of effort if same expertise exists in existing departments

Bangladesh Examples

Padma Bridge: Bangladesh government set up a dedicated project authority — Bridge Division — with its own PM, engineers, finance team, and HR. No existing department could handle this scale — dedicated structure used.

DMTCL (Dhaka Metro Rail): Dhaka Mass Transit Company Ltd is a completely dedicated entity — PM, engineers, procurement, HR all specifically hired for this long-term infrastructure project.
🔀 Matrix Structure ★★★☆
Exam TF 23-24, 21-22, 20-21, 18-19, 17-18, 16-17 — "Draw and explain the matrix structure" is a very common question. Know the 3 types and Bangladesh examples.

What is Matrix Structure?

Matrix Structure is a hybrid between Functional and Dedicated. It is used for short-term projects that are not specific to any single department. The PM is hired from outside on a contract basis. Team members are borrowed temporarily from various departments — they return to their departments once the project is complete.

Three Types of Matrix Structure

The key difference among the three matrix types is the balance of authority between PM and FM:

TypePM AuthorityFM AuthorityDept. OrientationBangladesh Example
Weak Matrix Low — PM only does Planning and Controlling High — FM does Organizing and Leading (also does main work) 50–60% dept-oriented Chaldal.com adding a minor new feature (local e-commerce app). Small scope → weak PM authority needed
Balanced Matrix Medium — PM and FM share authority equally Medium — both PM and FM have defined roles 50–50 City Bank developing a custom internet banking app — balanced between technical (FM) and project delivery (PM)
Heavy Matrix (Strong Matrix) High — PM does Planning, Organizing, Controlling, Leading. PM defines FM's authority Low — FM provides only technical expertise; authority defined by PM Project-oriented Government software system (NID or Passport system) — complex, cross-departmental, PM needs strong control

BrainStation 23 — Matrix Example (Bangladesh Software Company)

BrainStation 23 (Bangladeshi software development company) regularly uses Matrix Structure for client projects. When a client hires them for a software project:
• A PM is hired on contract from outside
• Developers, QA officers, designers from their existing departments are temporarily assigned (borrowed)
• After project delivery, those team members return to their departments
• This is classic Matrix — short-term, cross-functional, hybrid authority
📊 Comparison & Choosing a Structure ★★★☆

4 Criteria for Choosing an Organisational Structure

Exam TF 23-24, 21-22 — "How do you choose a project organisation structure?"
#CriterionWhat to consider
1 Potential Project Nature Is the project department-specific or cross-departmental? Long-term or short-term? If long-term → Dedicated. If cross-departmental & short-term → Matrix.
2 Organisation Characteristics What structures does the company currently have? A small startup may only have a Line structure — can it support a Dedicated department?
3 Each Structure's Pros and Cons Functional = cheap but risky for timelines. Dedicated = expensive but focused. Matrix = balance but can cause dual authority conflict.
4 Parent Organisation's Cultural Preference Some organisations have cultural or policy constraints. Example: Islamic Bank won't invest in projects that involve interest (sood), or in brands like Lux Superstar contest that may conflict with Islamic values.

Project Team Formation

Regardless of which structure is chosen, a project team has a standard formation: 1 PM + 2 types of departments — Technical and Logistics.
Technical Department roles:
1. Project Engineer — does the design, development, and initial planning work
2. Manufacturing Engineer — executes/implements the actual project work physically
3. Field Manager — handles installation, maintenance, and after-sales service (on-site)

Logistics Department roles:
1. Contract Administrator — official liaison; deals with customers, suppliers, and Government officials
2. Project Controller — manages finance and money; tracks budget, expenditure, cash flow
3. Support Service Manager — transportation, subcontractors, and general support functions

❓ Past Year Questions & Full Answers — AA Ch 2
These are complete model answers. Write them in the exam exactly as shown.

Q1. Draw and explain the matrix structure. Show PM and FM authority for all matrix types. [TF 23-24, 21-22, 20-21, 18-19 ★★★]

Answer:
Matrix Structure is a hybrid between Functional and Dedicated structures, used for short-term cross-departmental projects. It has dual authority — both PM (horizontal) and FM (vertical) over the borrowed team members.

Three types:
1. Weak Matrix: PM has low authority (Planning + Controlling only). FM handles Organizing, Leading, and actual work. 50–60% department-oriented. Example: Chaldal minor feature addition.

2. Balanced Matrix: PM and FM share authority equally. PM does Planning and Controlling; FM does Organizing and Leading. 50–50 orientation. Example: City Bank custom internet banking app.

3. Heavy (Strong) Matrix: PM has high authority (P, O, L, C all). FM provides only technical expertise, and their scope is defined by the PM. Project-oriented. Example: Government NID/Passport software system.

[Draw a matrix grid with FM columns and PM rows, showing borrowed staff at intersections. Include authority spectrum arrow from FM-dominant to PM-dominant.]

Q2. Compare Functional vs Dedicated project organisation. [TF 17-18, 14-15, 11-12 ★★★]

Functional Structure: No PM hired. Project is 80–90% department-oriented. FM does all 4 management functions (P, O, L, C). Advantages: low cost, existing expertise. Disadvantages: competing priorities, misses time & budget. Example: BRAC Bank payroll upgrade.

Dedicated Structure: Separate PM hired + all team members separately recruited. PM is all in all (P, O, L, C). FM helps with technical expertise only. High investment. Full focus on project. Best for long-term or frequent projects. Example: Padma Bridge, Dhaka Metro Rail.

Q3. What are the criteria for selecting an organisational structure? [TF 23-24, 21-22 ★★★]

4 Criteria:
1. Potential Project Nature — Is the project dept-specific or cross-functional? Long or short-term?
2. Organisation Characteristics — What existing structures does the organisation have?
3. Pros and Cons of each structure — Evaluate cost, focus, authority, and complexity of each option
4. Parent Organisation's Cultural Preference — Cultural, ethical, or policy constraints (Example: Islamic Bank won't invest in projects conflicting with Islamic values)

Q4. Describe the project team formation. [5 marks ★★]

Project Team = 1 PM + Technical Dept + Logistics Dept

Technical Department:
1. Project Engineer — design, development, planning
2. Manufacturing Engineer — physical execution of project
3. Field Manager — installation, maintenance, after-sales service

Logistics Department:
1. Contract Administrator — deals with customers, suppliers, Government officials
2. Project Controller — finance and money management
3. Support Service Manager — transportation, subcontractors, general support
