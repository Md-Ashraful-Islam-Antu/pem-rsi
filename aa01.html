📌 What is a Project? ★★★☆
Definition (Meredith & Mantel):
"A complex, non-routine, one-time effort limited by time, budget, resources and performance specifications designed to meet customer needs."

Project vs Function (Routine Operations)

The difference between a project and a regular function is the core exam question. The teacher explained it via 6 characteristics — Characteristic 1 applies to both; Characteristics 2–6 are exclusive to projects.

6 Characteristics of a Project

#CharacteristicExplanation (Teacher's Notes)Example
1 Established Objective
Applies to BOTH project & function		 Any task — project or function — must have a clearly established objective from the start. It is a Must. A factory producing markers also has an established objective (daily production quota)
2 Unique
Project exclusive		 Unique means something new & different. The project doesn't have to be 100% new — if it's completely new it's called an Innovation Project. Even at least one criteria (location, length, structure) makes it unique. Japan Garden City: each building is identical, but still a project because every building has a different location → Unique.
Factory markers: 10,000 identical markers produced → NOT a project.
3 Defined Life Span
Project exclusive		 A project has a fixed start and end date. Even if the end date isn't met, it was intended to end. A function has no definite end — it runs indefinitely. Factory marker production can continue for years indefinitely → function, NOT project.
4 Defined Budget
Project exclusive		 All projects have a known life span, so budgets are fixed. Even if budget increases due to scope creep, it was originally defined. Jamuna-Gomoti Setu: didn't finish within time & budget (Japanese construction company was hired). Function has no lifelong budget estimate.
5 Multifunctional (Cross-functional)
Project exclusive		 Projects bring together people from different backgrounds — EEE, Civil, MME, WRE, Finance, Accounts, HR etc. — in one team. Regular function stays within one department.

Conflict arises due to different backgrounds — but is conflict good or bad? (Discussed later.) A regular function cannot be called cross-functional.		 Building a bridge: EEE + Civil + MME + WRE + Finance + HR working together.
6 Uncertain
Project exclusive		 Projects are new/unique, so uncertainty is inherent. That means Risk is involved. Every project has a Risk Management Plan: how to mitigate, manage & overcome the risk. Regular functions have no uncertainty — so risk is not included. A new factory setup has uncertainty about delays, cost overruns, technical challenges.
Exam Note (TF 18-19, 13-14): "What are the characteristics that separate a project from a regular function? Justify." — Answer: Characteristics 2–6 above (Unique, Defined Life Span, Defined Budget, Multifunctional, Uncertain). Characteristic 1 (Established Objective) applies to both so it doesn't separate them. (7 marks)

Project Success Criteria — 3 Things a PM Must Have

When a project is complete, it is delivered to the customer. During execution, the PM must maintain 3 things to be considered successful:
#CriteriaExplanation
1 Responsibility The PM has a target and must ensure it is met — that is their responsibility. Example: If I'm given 20 lakh taka for event management, my target is to ensure the event happens right — that's my responsibility.
2 Authority The PM must have the authority to make decisions (e.g., spend money) without needing permission for every small thing. If I need boss approval for even 20 taka, I won't be able to deliver efficiently. I must have authority.
3 Accountability Even though the company gave me money and trust, I have accountability — I must give account to the company (top management). As an employee, I must be answerable for how resources were used.
🔄 Project Life Cycle ★★★☆ — Appeared in TF 20-21 (20 marks)
High exam topic. Draw the 4-stage curve and explain each stage. The teacher uses: Define → Plan → Execute → Deliver.

All projects go through four distinct stages. The Level of Effort vs. Time graph shows how resources peak and taper across the life cycle.

The Four Stages in Detail

StageName (Teacher)Effort LevelActivitiesKey Output
I Define Low — time & effort both minimal Identify need, feasibility study, rough scope, go/no-go decision, establish objective Project charter, feasibility report
II Plan Rising — effort level increases compared to Define Detailed planning, WBS, budget estimation, schedule creation, resource planning, risk analysis, team assembly Project plan, baseline schedule, budget
III Execute Peak — most time & physical labor needed here Actual work performed, monitoring, controlling, managing changes, corrective action Project deliverables
IV Deliver Declining — ramp-down after execution Project handover to customer, after-sales service, final reporting, team release, lessons learned Delivered project, final report, closed contract
Deliver stage length varies: Sometimes short (apartment handover — delivery stage is brief), sometimes long (factory machinery setup — maintenance service continues for years). So the Deliver stage can be lengthy.
Common exam trap: Risk is highest at Stage I (Define — high uncertainty, little known) but cost impact of changes is lowest then. At Stage III (Execute), risk events are most likely to actually occur. The Level of Effort peaks at Stage III.
🗂️ Types of Projects ★★☆☆

Classification by Need (Teacher's Classification — TF asked multiple times)

Exam TF refs: TF 14-15, TF 15-16, TF 16-17, TF 17-18 — project types by need/purpose. Know all 9 types with examples.
#TypeDefinitionExample
1 New Completely new construction — something that didn't exist before Building a new Road, Bridge, etc.
2 Balancing Same input → more output (increase efficiency). Input stays the same, output increases through productivity improvements Kaizen, Line Balancing, JIT technique use to increase factory productivity/efficiency
3 Expansion Increase/expand an existing project (add more capacity) Increasing production lines from 20 to 25
4 Modernization Update/upgrade an existing project while keeping it running Adding a controller to a manual machine to automate it; TF 14-15

8 Forward Integration Integrating downstream — becoming your own distributor/retailer Textile factory owner sets up a Garments Factory to use own fabric — integrates forward toward customer; TF 17-18
9 Turn Key A third party (3rd party) does everything — planning, construction, installation. You just "turn the key" and use it. You don't do anything yourself. Factory owner hires a 3rd party to install all machinery. Machine arrives ready to run — just turn the key.

🔺 The PM Triangle (Performance–Time–Budget) ★★★☆
Fundamental concept. Every project must balance three competing constraints. The teacher's version: Performance, Time, Budget — with Resources as the base.
Teacher's formula: Fixed Resource → within Time & Budget → show Performance.
i.e. with the fixed resources available, deliver the project within time and within budget, while maintaining performance (quality) up to customer satisfaction.

The Three Constraints Explained

ConstraintWhat it meansTools to Manage
PerformanceProject quality maintenance up to customer satisfaction; technical specification of deliverableWBS, scope statement, change control
Time (Schedule)When the project must be completed; milestones and deadlines; Time-boundedCPM/PERT, Gantt chart, crashing
Budget (Cost)The financial resources available; cost-boundedBudget, earned value, cost control
Key insight: PM's job is to balance all three. Crashing (shortening time) increases cost. Increasing scope increases both time and cost. Good PM prevents uncontrolled changes (scope creep).

SUCCESS Formula (Teacher's Framework)

How to make a project successful:
Select a dream (choose the most feasible idea)
Use that dream to set a goal (= Project Life Cycle Stage I: Define)
Create a plan (= Stage II: Plan)
Consider Resources (budget, manpower, time)
Enhance Knowledge & Skill (keep updating skills)
Spend time Wisely
Start

If we follow these activities before starting a project, the chance of success increases greatly.
👥 Group vs Team & Piecemeal System ★★★☆ — TF 23-24 (5 marks)
Exam topic TF 23-24, TF Jan 21, TF 16-17, TF 12-13. Group vs Team is a 5-mark question. Piecemeal System appears in multiple terms.

Group vs Team — Which is Better?

DimensionGroupTeam
Responsibility Each member is responsible only for their own work. If member C doesn't do their part, only C is responsible — the work won't complete. Responsibility is shared by everyone. Even if 1–2 members don't do their part, everyone else covers it and work gets completed.
Synergy 2 + 2 = 4 (no extra benefit) 2 + 2 = 5 (synergy) — Together Everyone Achieves More (TEAM)
Idea Generation Individual ideas only Better ideas generated through collaboration
Example A, B, C, D in a group — C doesn't work → work incomplete PD Lab: even if 1–2 people don't work, the work ultimately gets done
Conclusion: Team is best. TEAM = Together Everyone Achieves More

Piecemeal System

The Piecemeal System is a problem in organizations — it describes how traditional department structures PREVENT effective project work. Understanding it explains WHY we need cross-functional teams.

In any office, there are 3 types of management:

#LevelRole
1Top ManagementCEO, Directors, VP level — highest authority
2Middle ManagementDepartment managers — connect top and frontline
3Front-line ManagementSupervisors and officers who directly manage workers
Why Piecemeal is a problem:
In a Piecemeal System, the management officers don't have good informal relations with each other. Different departments (Engineering, Accounts, Finance, Production etc.) don't communicate well. When one department faces a problem, they look for solutions only within their own department.

Analogy: "Piecemeal" = eating food piece by piece (এক টুকরো করে খাওয়া). If you eat a meal piece by piece (not all together), you can't enjoy the food. Similarly, if a project is run with piecemeal system (each department working in silos), the business won't be successful.

Solution: Dissolve the Departmental Borders → Form a Team

Steps:
1. Take people from different departments (Engineering, Finance, Marketing, Production etc.)
2. Dissolve the departmental borders (remove silos)
3. Form a cross-functional TEAM
4. This converts "Piecemeal Group → PMS" into a TEAM
5. We already know: Team is best

When the project is complete, team members return to their own departments. After project success, the PMS (piecemeal system) must be removed from business operations.

Connection to Project Characteristics

This directly connects to Characteristic 5 (Multifunctional) of a project — projects are cross-functional by nature. The piecemeal system explains the problem that cross-functional teams solve. Conflict can arise in cross-functional teams (different backgrounds, different egos) — but healthy conflict leads to better ideas and decisions.

Q1. What are the characteristics that separate a project from a regular function? Justify. [7 marks, TF 18-19, TF 13-14 ★★★]

Answer:
Established Objective (Characteristic 1) applies to BOTH projects and regular functions — so it doesn't separate them. The characteristics that EXCLUSIVELY belong to a project (and thus separate it from regular functions) are characteristics 2–6:

1. Unique: Every project produces something new and different. Even if 100% new isn't required, at least one criteria (location, length, structure) must be unique. Example: Japan Garden City's identical buildings are still projects because each has a different location. Factory-made identical markers → NOT a project (no uniqueness).

2. Defined Life Span: Projects have a fixed start and end date. Regular functions run indefinitely (factory marker production never has a planned end date).

3. Defined Budget: Since a project's life span is known, its budget is also fixed/defined. Regular functions have no lifelong budget estimate — they run as long as the business runs.

4. Multifunctional: Projects require a cross-functional team — people from EEE, Civil, MME, WRE, Finance, Accounts, HR etc. Regular functions stay within one department. That's why conflict can arise in projects (different backgrounds) but not in routine departmental work.

5. Uncertain: Since projects are new/unique, uncertainty is inherent — which means Risk is involved. Every project has a Risk Management Plan. Regular functions are repetitive — no uncertainty, no risk management needed.

Q2. Describe the 4-stage project life cycle with a diagram. [TF 20-21 (20 marks) ★★★]

Answer:
The project life cycle shows how the Level of Effort changes over time throughout a project. All projects pass through four stages: Define → Plan → Execute → Deliver.

Stage I — Define: Low time and effort. The project idea is formulated, objective established, feasibility studied, and a go/no-go decision made. Scope is defined. Uncertainty is highest here.

Stage II — Plan: Effort level increases. Detailed planning occurs — WBS created, schedule set, budget finalized, teams assembled. Risk analysis done. Cost commitments begin rising.

Stage III — Execute: Peak effort and physical labor. Actual work is performed. The PM monitors progress, controls changes. This is the longest, most resource-intensive phase. Physical output is produced here.

Stage IV — Deliver: Ramp-down. Deliverables handed over to customer. After-sales service begins. Length varies — short for apartment handover, long for machinery installation/maintenance. Final reports written, team released, lessons learned documented.

Q3. What is a project? What are its characteristics? [5 marks ★★★]

Definition: A project is "a complex, non-routine, one-time effort limited by time, budget, resources and performance specifications designed to meet customer needs."

6 Characteristics:
1. Established Objective — Must have a clearly defined goal (applies to both project and function)
2. Unique — Something new and different; at least one criteria differs from past work
3. Defined Life Span — Has a fixed start and end; temporary not permanent
4. Defined Budget — Financial resources are planned and limited
5. Multifunctional — Cross-functional team (people from different departments/disciplines)
6. Uncertain — New/unique nature brings uncertainty → Risk involved → needs Risk Management Plan

Q4. Group vs Team — Which is better? Justify. [5 marks, TF 23-24 ★★★]

Answer:
Group: In a group, each member is responsible only for their own work. If one member (say C) doesn't do their part, the work won't complete. The responsibility is individual. Group: 2+2=4.

Team: In a team, responsibility is shared. Even if 1–2 members don't contribute, the remaining members ensure the work gets done. Team: 2+2=5 (synergy — Together Everyone Achieves More = TEAM). Teams generate better ideas through collaboration.

Conclusion: Team is better. Example: In PD Lab, even if 1–2 students don't work, the team ultimately completes the assignment.

Q5. Classify projects by need/purpose. [TF 16-17, 15-16, 14-15 ★★]

Answer: Projects can be classified into 9 types based on their need/purpose:
1. New — Creating something that never existed (new road, bridge)
2. Balancing — Same input, more output — improve efficiency (Kaizen, JIT, Line Balancing)
3. Expansion — Increase capacity of existing project (20 → 25 production lines)
4. Modernization — Update/automate existing project (add controller to manual machine)
5. Replacement — Remove old, bring new (manual → automatic machines)
6. Diversification — Expand into multiple business sectors to cover risk (Bashundhara, Akij)
7. Backward Integration — Become your own supplier (Textile owner → Spinning Mill)
8. Forward Integration — Become your own distributor (Textile → Garments Factory)
9. Turn Key — 3rd party does everything; just turn key and use

Q6. What is the PM Triangle? Explain with trade-offs. [5 marks ★★★]

Answer:
The PM Triangle (Iron Triangle) states every project is constrained by three linked factors: Performance, Time, Budget.

• Performance — Quality of deliverable up to customer satisfaction
• Time — Project must complete within the schedule (time-bounded)
• Budget — Must use resources within the fixed budget (cost-bounded)

Formula: Fixed Resources → within Time & Budget → show Performance.

Trade-offs:
— Add more features → need more time OR more money
— Compress schedule → need more money (crashing) OR reduce scope
— Cut budget → must reduce scope OR extend deadline

PM's job is to balance all three to meet customer expectations.
