Chapter Roadmap

📌 What is a Project? ★★★☆
A project is a specific, finite task to be accomplished — with a defined beginning and end — that is important enough to be managed separately. It involves commitment of resources over a defined time to achieve specific goals.

Project vs Routine Operations

ProjectRoutine Operations
Has a definite start and endOngoing, repetitive
Unique output or deliverableStandardized output
Temporary team assembledPermanent organization
High uncertainty and riskLower uncertainty
Examples: building a bridge, launching softwareExamples: payroll, manufacturing

🔄 Project Life Cycle ★★★☆ — 3× in PYQ
High exam topic. Draw and explain the 4 stages with their characteristics. Know what happens at each stage and when resources peak.

All projects go through four distinct stages. Each stage has different levels of resource use, risk, and stakeholder involvement.

The Four Stages in Detail

StageNameActivitiesKey Output
IConcept / InitiationNeed identification, feasibility study, rough scope, go/no-go decisionProject charter / Feasibility report
IIDevelopment / PlanningDetailed planning, WBS, budget, schedule, resource planning, risk analysisProject plan, baseline schedule
IIIExecution / ImplementationActual work performed, monitoring, controlling, managing changesProject deliverables
IVFinish / CloseoutTesting, handover, final reporting, team release, lessons learnedFinal report, closed contract
Common exam trap: Risk is highest at Stage I (high uncertainty, little known) but cost impact of changes is lowest then. At Stage III, risk events are most likely to occur.

Meredith & Mantel classify projects into several types based on their nature, objective, and technology involved.

Remember: PERT (probabilistic) is used for R&D/uncertain projects; CPM (deterministic) is used for construction/well-defined projects.
🔺 The PM Triangle ★★★☆
Fundamental concept. Every project must balance three competing constraints. Improving one typically worsens at least one other.

The Constraints Explained

ConstraintDefinitionTools to Manage
Scope / PerformanceThe work to be done; quality and technical specifications of the deliverableWBS, scope statement, change control
Time (Schedule)When the project must be completed; milestones and deadlinesCPM/PERT, Gantt chart, crashing
Cost (Budget)The financial resources available to complete the projectBudget, earned value, cost control
Key insight: PM's job is to balance all three. Crashing (shortening time) increases cost. Increasing scope increases both time and cost. Good PM prevents uncontrolled changes (scope creep).

Three Skills of a Successful PM

  • Skill to trade-off performance, time, and cost
  • Skill in continuous cost-benefit analysis
  • Ability to handle uncertainty in projects
