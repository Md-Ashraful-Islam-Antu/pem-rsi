Crashing is the process of compressing a project's schedule by adding resources (at extra cost) to critical path activities. The goal is to find the cheapest way to meet an earlier deadline.

Exam focus: Given a network with normal and crash time/cost, reduce duration step by step (always crash the cheapest critical activity). Show each stage's total project cost. This carries the most marks (15–22).
📚 Crashing Concepts ★★★☆
Crashing means reducing a project's duration by adding resources (more workers, overtime, better equipment) to activities on the critical path. This always increases direct costs but may reduce indirect costs (overhead, penalties).
Cost Slope Formula
Cost Slope = (CC − NC) / (NT − CT)
CC = Crash Cost (cost at maximum compression)
NC = Normal Cost (cost at normal duration)
NT = Normal Time (baseline duration)
CT = Crash Time (minimum possible duration)
Cost Slope = extra cost per day of reduction
Note: denominator is (NT − CT) because you're REDUCING time (NT > CT)
ConceptExplanation
Normal pointNormal time and cost — baseline conditions
Crash pointMinimum possible time and maximum direct cost
Direct costsLabor, materials — rise when you crash (overtime, extra workers)
Indirect costsOverhead, penalties — fall when you shorten the project
Optimal durationWhere total cost (direct + indirect) is minimized
Partial crashingReducing by less than the full crash amount (staying between normal and crash point)
🔧 Step-by-Step Crashing Procedure ★★★★
Always follow this procedure for crashing numericals.
StepAction
1Draw the network, find the critical path, calculate project duration
2Calculate cost slope for each activity = (CC−NC)/(NT−CT)
3Identify all activities on the critical path
4Among critical path activities, choose the one with the lowest cost slope
5Crash that activity by 1 day (or as many days as possible within its crash limit)
6Recalculate the network — the critical path may change!
7Record the new project duration and total cost
8Repeat from Step 3 until desired duration is reached
Critical: When you crash, the critical path may change.
• A new critical path may emerge (a previously non-critical path may become critical)
• When two paths become critical simultaneously, you must crash BOTH paths together
• Crashing stops when: (a) desired duration reached, or (b) no more crashing is cost-effective (direct cost gain < indirect cost saved)
Two Principles of Crashing:
1. Always crash on the critical path — crashing non-critical activities wastes money without reducing project duration
2. Among critical path activities, always crash the cheapest (lowest cost slope) first

Problem: Find the cheapest way to reduce the project from 15 days to 12 days. Indirect cost = $50/day.

Network Paths (normal durations)
Path 1: A→C→E = 5+6+3 = 14 days
Path 2: A→D→E = 5+4+3 = 12 days
Path 3: B→D→E = 8+4+3 = 15 days ← CRITICAL PATH
Initial project duration = 15 days. Normal total direct cost = 500+800+600+400+300 = $2,600
Stage 1: Reduce from 15 to 14 days
Critical path: B→D→E. Cost slopes: B=$150, D=$100, E=$120.
Cheapest: D ($100/day). Crash D by 1 day (D: 4→3).
New cost = $2,600 + $100 = $2,700
New paths: B→D→E = 8+3+3 = 14 days. A→C→E = 14 days. Both are now critical!
Stage 2: Reduce from 14 to 13 days
TWO critical paths: B→D→E and A→C→E.
Must reduce BOTH paths simultaneously.
Option 1: Crash E (on both paths) for $120 → reduces both by 1 day ✓
Option 2: Crash A+B ($100+$150=$250) or A+D ($100+$100=$200)...
Cheapest: Crash E ($120). E: 3→2.
New cost = $2,700 + $120 = $2,820
Duration = 13 days.
Stage 3: Reduce from 13 to 12 days
TWO critical paths: B→D→E and A→C→E.
E is fully crashed (CT=2, now at 2). Can't crash E more.
Must crash one activity on each path separately:
Path 1 (B→D→E): Options: B=$150, D=$100 (D already at 3, min is 2 so still can crash 1 more)
Path 2 (A→C→E): Options: A=$100, C=$150
Cheapest combination: Crash D (from path 1, $100) + Crash A (from path 2, $100) = $200 total
New cost = $2,820 + $200 = $3,020. Duration = 12 days. ✓
